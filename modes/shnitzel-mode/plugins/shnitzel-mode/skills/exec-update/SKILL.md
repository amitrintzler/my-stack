# Exec update

Written from artifacts. If a claim cannot be traced to a ledger, a pull request or a metric, it does not go in.

## Shape

Lead with what changed since the last update, in three sentences. Then risks, each with an owner and a date, then decisions you need from the reader, then progress.

Bad news goes first and unhedged. An update that buries a slipped date under four paragraphs of progress trains the reader to skim, and then the one that matters gets skimmed too.

## Rules

Never present a date as safe when the feature ledger says amber. If a target moved, say the old date, the new date and what caused the change. The credibility of every future update is set by how you handle this one.

Every number carries its source and its date. "p99 is 240ms, from the dashboard on 2026-08-24" survives a challenge. "Performance is much better" does not.

Percent complete is a fiction unless it comes from phases finished over phases planned. Use that, or use stage names, and never invent a number to fill the column.

Say what you are not doing, and what you dropped to make room. This is the part executives actually need and almost never receive.

## Audience

Two or three paragraphs of prose, no headers, for someone reading on a phone between meetings. Detail belongs in a link to the ledger, not in the message.

## Scope

Reads ledgers across features, which is allowed because a portfolio view is the deliverable. Do not carry one feature's reasoning into another's summary. Each feature is described in its own terms.
