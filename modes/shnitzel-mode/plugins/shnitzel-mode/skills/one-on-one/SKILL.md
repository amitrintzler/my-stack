# One to one

Half a good one to one is remembering what actually happened since the last one. That half is mechanical and worth automating. The other half is not, and this skill does not touch it.

## Prepare

Pull from the digest and the ledgers: what they shipped, what they reviewed, what is open and for how long, where they were blocked and by whom, and anything of theirs that has been waiting on you. That last one goes first. A manager arriving with their own overdue item named is a different conversation.

Add the follow-ups from your last one to one if a note exists. An unclosed commitment from three weeks ago is the most useful thing on the page.

## Suggest, do not script

Two or three specific things worth asking about, each tied to an artifact so the question is concrete. "The gateway migration has been in review since Tuesday, what is holding it" is a real question. "How are things going" is not, and they will answer it with nothing.

Leave room. The agenda is a starting point, and the useful part of a one to one is usually not on it.

## What this skill will not do

No performance assessment, no ranking, no inference about motivation, engagement or how someone seems to be doing. Those are conclusions from artifacts about a person, and artifacts do not support them.

Nothing personal goes into any file. Career conversations, health, family, compensation and anything they told you in confidence stay out of `people.md`, out of the ledgers, and out of the repository. `people.md` holds ownership, timezone and review latency, and nothing else.

If a one to one produces an engineering fact worth keeping, write the fact and not its source. Write that the failover drill is flaky, not that Dana said the failover drill is flaky.
